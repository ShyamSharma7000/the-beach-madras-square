# Pitch notes: The Beach by Madras Square

Private to this project. Edit freely.

## Before you go

- Treat this as a concept you made on spec. Say so. Do not call it their website.
- Open it on your phone, not a laptop. Most of their guests will see it that way.
- Have it running from a private Vercel preview so it loads fast and you aren't depending on
  venue Wi-Fi. Have `npm run start` ready locally as a backup.
- Find out who decides: owner, general manager, or marketing. Ask for 15 minutes with that person.

## The 60-second demo

1. Open on the hero. Let the text rise, then say nothing for a second.
2. Hand them the phone. Tell them to drag the sun. This is the moment that sells it. Guests
   will feel the place before they arrive.
3. Scroll to the menu. Tap through the four cuisines. Say: "These slots fill with your real
   dishes and prices from one file. Updating a price takes a minute."
4. Open the gallery. Say: "These are placeholders. Your photography goes here, and it carries
   the whole page."
5. Tap Reserve. It opens their real booking page. Say: "I didn't build a booking system. Your
   existing one stays, guests just get to it from a better first impression."

## What they actually get

- A single-venue site designed around The Beach, not a restaurant template.
- Mobile-first, fast, accessible, and readable with animation switched off.
- Menu, prices, hours and booking link editable in one data file.
- Honest content: no invented reviews, ratings or awards.

## Say it plainly, and don't overclaim

- Don't promise more bookings or revenue. You have no data. Say it gives them a stronger first
  impression than a listing page and a place to send people from Instagram and Google.
- The site does not take reservations itself. It links to their booking page.
- The photos, menu, hours and logo have to come from them. The site is only as good as those.

## What you need from them to go live

- Original high-resolution photos and a few vertical videos, with written permission to use them.
- Logo files and brand preferences.
- Current menu with prices, current opening hours, confirmed phone and booking link.
- Official social account URLs.
- Written approval to present this as their site, and a domain (or permission to use a subdomain).

## Questions to ask them

- What do guests ask on the phone most often? (Hours, parking, menu, group bookings?)
- Which occasions bring the most revenue: sunsets, weekends, celebrations, corporate?
- Who updates the menu today, and how often?
- Do they already have a website vendor or contract that this would replace?

## Likely objections

- "We already have a website." Ask what it does for a guest on a phone at 6 pm deciding where to
  eat. Show the difference, don't argue.
- "Is it expensive?" Quote a fixed scope, and keep the booking engine and CMS out of the first release.
- "Can you add X?" Add it if it's real (events, private dining). Don't add features they don't offer.

## Next step to close

Offer a short paid first phase: photo shoot plan plus a launch-ready build with their real
content, delivered to a private preview for approval before any domain is connected.
